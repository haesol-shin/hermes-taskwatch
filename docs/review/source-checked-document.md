# Source-checked document review

Use this file with the common guidelines when the deliverable cites sources: a research note, a summary, a comparison, or a survey.

The aim is to catch a number, a quotation, or a conclusion that the sources do not support.

## What to review

**S-1.** Every claim that states a fact has a source passage a reviewer can find. A claim with no passage is a finding. Mark a passage you did not open as unverified.

**S-2.** Numbers, dates, and quotations match the source. A mismatch is a finding even when the surrounding prose is accurate.

**S-3.** Do not let the deliverable claim more than the source claims. A stronger conclusion, a dropped limit, or a maybe turned into a fact is a finding.

## Coverage

**S-4.** When a claim is about a source as a whole, the review has to cover that source, not only a summary or an excerpt of it. If you did not read the whole source, mark the finding unverified. Do not pass the claim as checked.

**S-5.** Check what is missing or vague, not only whether the written sentences are true. A true sentence can still hide the point the question asked for.

**S-6.** Keep distinctions the source keeps. Do not treat an example in the source as the setting of the whole claim. A name for a difference that does not say what the difference is does not count as a distinction.

**S-7.** When the deliverable compares items, and the source gives numbers for that comparison, leaving those numbers out is a finding.

**S-8.** A one-line summary states the finding, not only the topic. A summary that names the subject and stops is not a finding.
